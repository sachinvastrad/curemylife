1. Executive Summary 
HomeoOpinion is a digital health portal designed to democratise access to qualified 
homoeopathic second opinions. The platform connects patients who are seeking alternative or 
supplementary medical perspectives with BHMS/MD(Hom.) registered practitioners across India 
and globally. Patients upload lab reports, describe symptoms and medical history using 
structured homoeopathic intake forms, and receive an AI-generated preliminary case analysis. 
Licensed homoeopathic doctors then review the AI report, add their expert commentary, and 
offer follow-up consultations (online or in-clinic). An administrative layer handles doctor 
onboarding, case routing, compliance, and analytics. 
 
The portal is purpose-built for homoeopathy, incorporating domain-specific concepts such as 
constitutional case-taking, miasmatic assessment, modality capture, mental-emotional symptom 
profiling, and repertorization support — none of which exist in generic telemedicine platforms. 
 
2. Market Context and Opportunity 
India has over 2,24,000 registered homoeopathic doctors, 195 undergraduate and 43 
postgraduate colleges, and more than 7,800 government dispensaries offering homoeopathic 
services. Despite this vast infrastructure, patients in tier-2 and tier-3 cities often struggle to 
access specialist homoeopathic opinion, particularly in branches like psychiatry, paediatrics, and 
constitutional prescribing. 
 
The global medical second opinion market is experiencing rapid growth, with approximately 
38% of patients now using virtual consultations for second opinions. AI-powered diagnostic 
support is improving clinical precision by up to 20%, and 20% of clinicians already consult large 
language models for supplementary opinions. The Indian government’s Telemedicine Practice 
Guidelines (2020) explicitly cover Registered Homoeopathic Practitioners (RHPs), providing a 
clear regulatory framework. This convergence of digital health adoption, regulatory clarity, and 
an underserved patient population creates a strong opportunity for a homoeopathy-focused 
second opinion portal.

3.2 High-Level Flow 
1.​ Patient registers/logs in and creates a new case 
2.​ Patient fills structured homoeopathic intake form and uploads lab reports/documents 
3.​ AI engine generates a preliminary case analysis report (symptom extraction, possible 
rubrics, constitutional indicators) 
4.​ Case is routed to qualified doctors based on speciality tags and filters 
5.​ Doctor reviews the AI report plus uploaded documents, adds expert comments and 
recommendations 
6.​ Patient receives notification that the report is ready (WhatsApp/email/SMS) 
7.​ Patient reviews the report; if deeper consultation is desired, patient pays and books an 
appointment 
8.​ Doctor conducts consultation (video/audio/in-clinic), prescribes remedy, and documents 
follow-up plan 
9.​ Patient can return for follow-up appointments with the same doctor on the same case

4.2 Homoeopathy-Specific Case Intake Form 
Unlike generic medical platforms, HomeoOpinion captures data essential for homoeopathic 
case analysis. The intake form is structured across the following sections: 
 
4.2.1 Chief Complaint and History 
•​
Free-text description of current problem (what, when, how it started) 
•​
Duration and progression of symptoms 
•​
Any previous allopathic/homoeopathic/ayurvedic treatment taken and its effects 
•​
Family medical history (hereditary tendencies — relevant for miasmatic assessment) 
•​
Past significant illnesses, surgeries, vaccinations 
 
4.2.2 Constitutional and Mental-Emotional Profile 
This section captures the holistic picture that homoeopathic case-taking requires, going beyond 
just physical symptoms: 
•​
Temperament and disposition (anxious, irritable, weepy, reserved, sociable, etc.) 
•​
Fears and phobias (darkness, being alone, heights, disease, death, thunder, etc.) 
•​
Sleep patterns: position, dreams (recurring themes), quality, disturbances 
•​
Emotional triggers and coping style 
•​
Response to consolation, sympathy, contradiction 
•​
Food cravings and aversions (salt, sweet, sour, spicy, eggs, meat, milk, etc.) 
•​
Thermal preference: chilly or warm-blooded patient 
•​
Thirst pattern: quantity, frequency, temperature preference 
•​
Perspiration: location, timing, odour 
 
4.2.3 Modalities (Aggravating and Ameliorating Factors) 
•​
Time modalities: symptoms worse/better morning, evening, night, specific hours 
•​
Weather modalities: effect of heat, cold, humidity, storm, season change 
•​
Position modalities: sitting, standing, lying down, specific side 
•​
Activity: rest vs. motion, before/during/after exertion 
•​
Other: eating, pressure, touch, bathing, clothing

4.3 AI-Generated Preliminary Report 
The AI engine processes the intake form and uploaded documents to generate a preliminary 
analysis. This is explicitly positioned as a decision-support tool, not a diagnosis, in compliance 
with regulatory guidelines:

1.​ Symptom Extraction and Structuring: Converts free-text narrative into structured 
symptom categories (mental, general, particular) following Kent’s hierarchy 
2.​ Lab Report Parsing: Extracts key biomarkers, flags abnormal values, and presents them 
alongside homoeopathic relevance notes 
3.​ Rubric Suggestions: Maps reported symptoms to potential repertory rubrics (using 
standard rubric language from Kent’s Repertory) 
4.​ Constitutional Indicators: Based on the mental-emotional profile, food preferences, 
thermal state, and modalities, suggests possible constitutional types for the doctor’s 
consideration 
5.​ Miasmatic Tendency: Based on family history and symptom patterns, indicates possible 
predominant miasm (Psora, Sycosis, Syphilis, Tubercular) 
6.​ Red Flag Alerts: Identifies symptoms requiring urgent conventional medical attention, 
ensuring patient safety 
 
Disclaimer prominently displayed: “This AI-generated report is a preliminary analysis tool to 
assist qualified homoeopathic practitioners. It is not a diagnosis or prescription. All clinical 
decisions rest with the reviewing doctor.”

5.3 Case Dashboard and Review Workflow 
1.​ Doctor sees a filtered queue of incoming cases matching their speciality tags 
2.​ Each case card shows: patient demographics (anonymised until accepted), chief 
complaint summary, speciality tags, AI confidence score, submission timestamp 
3.​ Doctor can preview the AI-generated report and uploaded documents before accepting 
the case 
4.​ Upon accepting, the doctor gets full access to the patient’s case file 
5.​ Doctor studies the case, cross-references with materia medica knowledge, and adds 
structured comments: agreement/disagreement with AI suggestions, additional rubrics 
identified, recommended remedies (with potency and rationale), suggested 
investigations or lifestyle modifications, red flag observations 
6.​ Doctor dispatches the completed report back to the patient 
7.​ Case status updates to “Report Ready” and patient is notified

6. Admin Module 
6.1 Doctor Lifecycle Management 
1.​ Onboarding: review submitted credentials, verify against NRH/State Board records, 
approve or reject with reason 
2.​ Active monitoring: track each doctor’s response time, case acceptance rate, patient 
ratings, and complaint history 
3.​ Off-boarding: suspend or permanently remove doctors with documented reasons; handle 
pending cases and patient communication 
 
6.2 Case Visibility and Routing Controls 
•​
Admin can control which doctors see which cases (e.g., restrict sensitive cases, route to 
senior practitioners) 
•​
Auto-routing rules engine: match case speciality tags to doctor speciality tags with 
configurable weighting 
•​
Manual override: admin can reassign a case to a specific doctor 
•​
Escalation workflow: if no doctor accepts a case within the SLA window, admin is alerted 
to intervene

7. AI Engine Specifications 
The AI layer is the core differentiator of HomeoOpinion. It is purpose-trained for homoeopathic 
contexts, not a generic medical AI: 
 
7.1 Capabilities 
1.​ Lab Report OCR and Parser: Extract structured data from scanned blood/imaging 
reports using computer vision and NLP. Handle Indian lab formats (Thyrocare, SRL, Dr 
Lal PathLabs, etc.). 
2.​ Symptom-to-Rubric Mapping: Map patient-described symptoms to standard 
homoeopathic repertory rubrics. Support Kent’s Repertory rubric language as the 
baseline, with extensibility for Synthesis and Complete Repertory.

3.​ Constitutional Profiling: Based on mental, general, and particular symptoms, suggest 
possible constitutional remedy groups for the doctor’s consideration. 
4.​ Miasmatic Analysis: Analyse family history patterns and current symptom characteristics 
to suggest predominant miasmatic tendency (Psora, Sycosis, Syphilis, Tubercular). 
5.​ Red Flag Detection: Identify emergency or serious symptoms (chest pain, neurological 
deficits, suspected malignancies) that require urgent conventional medical attention, 
regardless of the homoeopathic context. 
6.​ Multi-lingual Support: Process intake forms in English, Hindi, and major regional 
languages; output reports in the user’s preferred language. 
 
7.2 Guardrails and Compliance 
•​
AI output is always labelled as “preliminary analysis” and “for practitioner review only” 
•​
AI never prescribes remedies or potencies directly to patients 
•​
Confidence scores accompany every AI suggestion, allowing doctors to gauge reliability 
•​
All AI outputs are logged and auditable for quality assurance and model improvement 
•​
Model bias monitoring: regular evaluation to ensure balanced performance across 
demographics 
 
8. Regulatory and Compliance Framework 
8.1 Indian Regulations 
•​
CCH Telemedicine Practice Guidelines (2020) for Registered Homoeopathic 
Practitioners: Governs all teleconsultation activity on the platform 
•​
Information Technology Act 2000 and IT Rules 2011: Data protection and intermediary 
liability 
•​
Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 (DPDPA): Consent management, data 
minimisation, purpose limitation, breach notification 
•​
Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940: E-prescription compliance for homoeopathic medicines 
•​
Clinical Establishments Act 2010: If applicable to partner clinics offering offline 
consultations 
•​
ABDM (Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission) standards: Future integration with national 
health ID ecosystem 
 
8.2 Data Protection Measures 
•​
All patient health information (PHI) encrypted at rest (AES-256) and in transit (TLS 1.3) 
•​
Role-based access control (RBAC): doctors see only cases assigned to them 
•​
Audit trails for all data access, modifications, and exports
